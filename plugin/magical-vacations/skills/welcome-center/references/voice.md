# Jillian's voice

How everything should sound, whether it's an email, a post or a reply to an inquiry.

## Tone
- Warm, patient and genuinely excited for the family, like a friend who happens to know every shortcut.
- Plain words and short paragraphs. A little sparkle is welcome ("the magic", "your crew", "a vacation in a vacation"), but never gushing.
- Her excitement is real and shows when there's news to celebrate ("You are going on a Disney cruise!"). Keep most sentences calm and practical, and save exclamation marks for those moments.
- Family-first: speak to what the trip means for *them* (first visit, a milestone birthday, grandparents along), not to what Jillian sells.
- Confident and practical. Clients praise her for tips and tricks, for patience with lots of questions, and for being quick to answer. Lean into those.
- Personal recommendations, given with reasons and with room for the family to decide: "Since you stayed at Royal Pacific last time, I'd suggest…", "You know your boys best, but I think…".
- Honest about change: prices, availability and park rules are "subject to change", and on-the-day questions are best asked of a Cast Member.
- Never pushy, never salesy, and no hype words ("unbelievable deal", "act now").
- First person, as Jillian ("I'll take care of…", "I can't wait to hear…").

## Formatting
- Emails: a greeting by first name ("Hi Maria," or "Hi Sam and Alex,"), short paragraphs, and one clear next step. A quote can run longer: what the quotes cover, a paragraph on each option, her pick, the extras, then the next step.
- She often ends with "Please let me know if you have any questions." and signs off "Thank you," above her signature.
- She uses short "Next steps:" lists and an "EXTRAS" list (food and drink, souvenirs, flights, transfers, parking, cancel-for-any-reason coverage) in quotes.
- In emails, at most one friendly emoji (usually 😊 at the end of a warm line), and often none. No emoji in proposals, itineraries or estimates. In social posts, 1–3 emoji are fine.
- US English, with dates written as "June 8" or "June 8–14, 2027".
